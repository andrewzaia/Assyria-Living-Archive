DNG 1:1 In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.
DNG 1:2 The Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, with part of the vessels of the house of God; and he carried them into the land of Shinar to the house of his god. He brought the vessels into the treasure house of his god.
DNG 1:3 The king spoke to Ashpenaz the master of his eunuchs, that he should bring in some of the children of Israel, even of the royal offspring and of the nobles—
DNG 1:4 youths in whom was no defect, but well-favoured, and skilful in all wisdom, and endowed with knowledge, and understanding science, and who had the ability to serve in the king’s palace; and that he should teach them the learning and the language of the Chaldeans.
DNG 1:5 The king appointed for them a daily portion of the king’s delicacies, and of the wine which he drank, and that they should be nourished three years; that at its end they should serve the king.
DNG 1:6 Now amongst these were of the children of Judah: Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.
DNG 1:7 The prince of the eunuchs gave names to them: to Daniel he gave the name Belteshazzar; to Hananiah, Shadrach; to Mishael, Meshach; and to Azariah, Abednego.
DNG 1:8 But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the king’s delicacies, nor with the wine which he drank. Therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself.
DNG 1:9 Now God made Daniel find kindness and compassion in the sight of the prince of the eunuchs.
DNG 1:10 The prince of the eunuchs said to Daniel, “I fear my lord the king, who has appointed your food and your drink. For why should he see your faces worse looking than the youths who are of your own age? Then you would endanger my head with the king.”
DNG 1:11 Then Daniel said to the steward whom the prince of the eunuchs had appointed over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah:
DNG 1:12 “Test your servants, I beg you, ten days; and let them give us vegetables to eat and water to drink.
DNG 1:13 Then let our faces be examined before you, and the face of the youths who eat of the king’s delicacies; and as you see, deal with your servants.”
DNG 1:14 So he listened to them in this matter, and tested them for ten days.
DNG 1:15 At the end of ten days, their faces appeared fairer, and they were fatter in flesh, than all the youths who ate of the king’s delicacies.
DNG 1:16 So the steward took away their delicacies, and the wine that they would drink, and gave them vegetables.
DNG 1:17 Now as for these four youths, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom; and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.
DNG 1:18 At the end of the days which the king had appointed for bringing them in, the prince of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.
DNG 1:19 The king talked with them; and amongst them all was found no one like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. Therefore they served the king.
DNG 1:20 In every matter of wisdom and understanding concerning which the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters who were in all his realm.
DNG 1:21 Daniel continued serving even to the first year of King Cyrus.
DNG 2:1 In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar dreamt dreams; and his spirit was troubled, and his sleep went from him.
DNG 2:2 Then the king commanded that the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans be called to tell the king his dreams. So they came in and stood before the king.
DNG 2:3 The king said to them, “I have dreamt a dream, and my spirit is troubled to know the dream.”
DNG 2:4 Then the Chaldeans spoke to the king in the Syrian language, “O king, live forever! Tell your servants the dream, and we will show the interpretation.”
DNG 2:5 The king answered the Chaldeans, “The thing has gone from me. If you don’t make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be cut in pieces, and your houses will be made a dunghill.
DNG 2:6 But if you show the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts, rewards, and great honour. Therefore show me the dream and its interpretation.”
DNG 2:7 They answered the second time and said, “Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will show the interpretation.”
DNG 2:8 The king answered, “I know of a certainty that you are trying to gain time, because you see the thing has gone from me.
DNG 2:9 But if you don’t make known to me the dream, there is but one law for you; for you have prepared lying and corrupt words to speak before me, until the situation changes. Therefore tell me the dream, and I will know that you can show me its interpretation.”
DNG 2:10 The Chaldeans answered before the king, and said, “There is not a man on the earth who can show the king’s matter, because no king, lord, or ruler, has asked such a thing of any magician, enchanter, or Chaldean.
DNG 2:11 It is a rare thing that the king requires, and there is no other who can show it before the king, except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh.”
DNG 2:12 Because of this, the king was angry and very furious, and commanded that all the wise men of Babylon be destroyed.
DNG 2:13 So the decree went out, and the wise men were to be slain. They sought Daniel and his companions to be slain.
DNG 2:14 Then Daniel returned answer with counsel and prudence to Arioch the captain of the king’s guard, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.
DNG 2:15 He answered Arioch the king’s captain, “Why is the decree so urgent from the king?” Then Arioch made the thing known to Daniel.
DNG 2:16 Daniel went in, and desired of the king that he would appoint him a time, and he would show the king the interpretation.
DNG 2:17 Then Daniel went to his house and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions,
DNG 2:18 that they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret, and that Daniel and his companions would not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.
DNG 2:19 Then the secret was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.
DNG 2:20 Daniel answered, “Blessed be the name of God forever and ever; for wisdom and might are his.
DNG 2:21 He changes the times and the seasons. He removes kings and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise, and knowledge to those who have understanding.
DNG 2:22 He reveals the deep and secret things. He knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him.
DNG 2:23 I thank you and praise you, O God of my fathers, who have given me wisdom and might, and have now made known to me what we desired of you; for you have made known to us the king’s matter.”
DNG 2:24 Therefore Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said this to him: “Don’t destroy the wise men of Babylon. Bring me in before the king, and I will show to the king the interpretation.”
DNG 2:25 Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said this to him: “I have found a man of the children of the captivity of Judah who will make known to the king the interpretation.”
DNG 2:26 The king answered Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, “Are you able to make known to me the dream which I have seen, and its interpretation?”
DNG 2:27 Daniel answered before the king, and said, “The secret which the king has demanded can’t be shown to the king by wise men, enchanters, magicians, or soothsayers;
DNG 2:28 but there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets, and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days. Your dream, and the visions of your head on your bed, are these:
DNG 2:29 “As for you, O king, your thoughts came on your bed, what should happen hereafter; and he who reveals secrets has made known to you what will happen.
DNG 2:30 But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but to the intent that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and that you may know the thoughts of your heart.
DNG 2:31 “You, O king, saw, and behold, a great image. This image, which was mighty, and whose brightness was excellent, stood before you; and its appearance was terrifying.
DNG 2:32 As for this image, its head was of fine gold, its chest and its arms of silver, its belly and its thighs of bronze,
DNG 2:33 its legs of iron, its feet part of iron, and part of clay.
DNG 2:34 You saw until a stone was cut out without hands, which struck the image on its feet that were of iron and clay, and broke them in pieces.
DNG 2:35 Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold were broken in pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshing floors. The wind carried them away, so that no place was found for them. The stone that struck the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth.
DNG 2:36 “This is the dream; and we will tell its interpretation before the king.
DNG 2:37 You, O king, are king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the strength, and the glory.
DNG 2:38 Wherever the children of men dwell, he has given the animals of the field and the birds of the sky into your hand, and has made you rule over them all. You are the head of gold.
DNG 2:39 “After you, another kingdom will arise that is inferior to you; and a third kingdom of bronze, which will rule over all the earth.
DNG 2:40 The fourth kingdom will be strong as iron, because iron breaks in pieces and subdues all things; and as iron that crushes all these, it will break in pieces and crush.
DNG 2:41 Whereas you saw the feet and toes, part of potters’ clay, and part of iron, it will be a divided kingdom; but there will be in it of the strength of the iron, because you saw the iron mixed with miry clay.
DNG 2:42 As the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom will be partly strong, and partly brittle.
DNG 2:43 Whereas you saw the iron mixed with miry clay, they will mingle themselves with the seed of men; but they won’t cling to one another, even as iron does not mix with clay.
DNG 2:44 “In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom which will never be destroyed, nor will its sovereignty be left to another people; but it will break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it will stand forever.
DNG 2:45 Because you saw that a stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold. The great God has made known to the king what will happen hereafter. The dream is certain, and its interpretation sure.”
DNG 2:46 Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face, worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an offering and sweet odours to him.
DNG 2:47 The king answered to Daniel, and said, “Of a truth your God is the God of gods, and the Lord of kings, and a revealler of secrets, since you have been able to reveal this secret.”
DNG 2:48 Then the king made Daniel great, and gave him many great gifts, and made him rule over the whole province of Babylon, and to be chief governor over all the wise men of Babylon.
DNG 2:49 Daniel requested of the king, and he appointed Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego over the affairs of the province of Babylon; but Daniel was in the king’s gate.
DNG 3:1 Nebuchadnezzar the king made an image of gold, whose height was sixty cubits, and its width six cubits. He set it up in the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.
DNG 3:2 Then Nebuchadnezzar the king sent to gather together the local governors, the deputies, and the governors, the judges, the treasurers, the counsellors, the sheriffs, and all the rulers of the provinces, to come to the dedication of the image which Nebuchadnezzar the king had set up.
DNG 3:3 Then the local governors, the deputies, and the governors, the judges, the treasurers, the counsellors, the sheriffs, and all the rulers of the provinces, were gathered together to the dedication of the image that Nebuchadnezzar the king had set up; and they stood before the image that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
DNG 3:4 Then the herald cried aloud, “To you it is commanded, peoples, nations, and languages,
DNG 3:5 that whenever you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music, you fall down and worship the golden image that Nebuchadnezzar the king has set up.
DNG 3:6 Whoever doesn’t fall down and worship shall be cast into the middle of a burning fiery furnace the same hour.”
DNG 3:7 Therefore at that time, when all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music, all the peoples, the nations, and the languages, fell down and worshipped the golden image that Nebuchadnezzar the king had set up.
DNG 3:8 Therefore at that time certain Chaldeans came near, and brought accusation against the Jews.
DNG 3:9 They answered Nebuchadnezzar the king, “O king, live for ever!
DNG 3:10 You, O king, have made a decree that every man who hears the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music shall fall down and worship the golden image;
DNG 3:11 and whoever doesn’t fall down and worship shall be cast into the middle of a burning fiery furnace.
DNG 3:12 There are certain Jews whom you have appointed over the affairs of the province of Babylon: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. These men, O king, have not respected you. They don’t serve your gods, and don’t worship the golden image which you have set up.”
DNG 3:13 Then Nebuchadnezzar in rage and fury commanded that Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego be brought. Then these men were brought before the king.
DNG 3:14 Nebuchadnezzar answered them, “Is it on purpose, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, that you don’t serve my god, nor worship the golden image which I have set up?
DNG 3:15 Now if you are ready whenever you hear the sound of the horn, flute, zither, lyre, harp, pipe, and all kinds of music to fall down and worship the image which I have made, good; but if you don’t worship, you shall be cast the same hour into the middle of a burning fiery furnace. Who is that god who will deliver you out of my hands?”
DNG 3:16 Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered the king, “Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter.
DNG 3:17 If it happens, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace; and he will deliver us out of your hand, O king.
DNG 3:18 But if not, let it be known to you, O king, that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image which you have set up.”
DNG 3:19 Then Nebuchadnezzar was full of fury, and the form of his appearance was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. He spoke, and commanded that they should heat the furnace seven times more than it was usually heated.
DNG 3:20 He commanded certain mighty men who were in his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, and to cast them into the burning fiery furnace.
DNG 3:21 Then these men were bound in their pants, their tunics, their mantles, and their other clothes, and were cast into the middle of the burning fiery furnace.
DNG 3:22 Therefore because the king’s commandment was urgent, and the furnace exceedingly hot, the flame of the fire killed those men who took up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.
DNG 3:23 These three men, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, fell down bound into the middle of the burning fiery furnace.
DNG 3:24 They walked in the midst of the fire, praising God, and blessing the Lord.
DNG 3:25 Then Azarias stood, and prayed like this. Opening his mouth in the midst of the fire he said,
DNG 3:26 “Blessed are you, O Lord, you God of our fathers! Your name is worthy to be praised and glorified for evermore;
DNG 3:27 for you are righteous in all the things that you have done. Yes, all your works are true. Your ways are right, and all your judgements are truth.
DNG 3:28 In all the things that you have brought upon us, and upon the holy city of our fathers, Jerusalem, you have executed true judgements. For according to truth and justice you have brought all these things upon us because of our sins.
DNG 3:29 For we have sinned and committed iniquity in departing from you.
DNG 3:30 In all things we have trespassed, and not obeyed your commandments or kept them. We haven’t done as you have commanded us, that it might go well with us.
DNG 3:31 Therefore all that you have brought upon us, and everything that you have done to us, you have done in true judgement.
DNG 3:32 You delivered us into the hands of lawless enemies, most hateful rebels, and to an unjust king who is the most wicked in all the world.
DNG 3:33 And now we can’t open our mouth. Shame and reproach have come on your servants and those who worship you.
DNG 3:34 Don’t utterly deliver us up, for your name’s sake. Don’t annul your covenant.
DNG 3:35 Don’t cause your mercy to depart from us, for the sake of Abraham who is loved by you, and for the sake of Isaac your servant, and Israel your holy one,
DNG 3:36 to whom you promised that you would multiply their offspring as the stars of the sky, and as the sand that is on the sea shore.
DNG 3:37 For we, O Lord, have become less than any nation, and are brought low this day in all the world because of our sins.
DNG 3:38 There isn’t at this time prince, or prophet, or leader, or burnt offering, or sacrifice, or oblation, or incense, or place to offer before you, and to find mercy.
DNG 3:39 Nevertheless in a contrite heart and a humble spirit let us be accepted,
DNG 3:40 like the burnt offerings of rams and bullocks, and like ten thousands of fat lambs. So let our sacrifice be in your sight this day, that we may wholly go after you, for they shall not be ashamed who put their trust in you.
DNG 3:41 And now we follow you with all our heart. We fear you, and seek your face.
DNG 3:42 Put us not to shame; but deal with us after your kindness, and according to the multitude of your mercy.
DNG 3:43 Deliver us also according to your marvellous works, and give glory to your name, O Lord. Let all those who harm your servants be confounded.
DNG 3:44 Let them be ashamed of all their power and might, and let their strength be broken.
DNG 3:45 Let them know that you are the Lord, the only God, and glorious over the whole world.”
DNG 3:46 The king’s servants who put them in didn’t stop making the furnace hot with naphtha, pitch, tinder, and small wood,
DNG 3:47 so that the flame streamed out forty nine cubits above the furnace.
DNG 3:48 It spread and burnt those Chaldeans whom it found around the furnace.
DNG 3:49 But the angel of the Lord came down into the furnace together with Azarias and his fellows, and he struck the flame of the fire out of the furnace,
DNG 3:50 and made the midst of the furnace as it had been a moist whistling wind, so that the fire didn’t touch them at all. It neither hurt nor troubled them.

DNG 3:91 Then Nebuchadnezzar the king was astonished and rose up in haste. He spoke and said to his counsellors, “Didn’t we cast three men bound into the middle of the fire?” They answered the king, “True, O king.”
DNG 3:92 He answered, “Look, I see four men loose, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are unharmed. The appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods.”

DNG 3:97 Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego in the province of Babylon.
DNG 4:1 Nebuchadnezzar the king, to all the peoples, nations, and languages, who dwell in all the earth: Peace be multiplied to you.
DNG 4:2 It has seemed good to me to show the signs and wonders that the Most High God has worked towards me.
DNG 4:3 How great are his signs! How mighty are his wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom. His dominion is from generation to generation.
DNG 4:4 I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at rest in my house, and flourishing in my palace.
DNG 4:5 I saw a dream which made me afraid; and the thoughts on my bed and the visions of my head troubled me.
DNG 4:6 Therefore I made a decree to bring in all the wise men of Babylon before me, that they might make known to me the interpretation of the dream.
DNG 4:7 Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans, and the soothsayers came in; and I told the dream before them; but they didn’t make known to me its interpretation.
DNG 4:8 But at the last Daniel came in before me, whose name was Belteshazzar, according to the name of my god, and in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. I told the dream before him, saying,
DNG 4:9 “Belteshazzar, master of the magicians, because I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you, and no secret troubles you, tell me the visions of my dream that I have seen, and its interpretation.
DNG 4:10 These were the visions of my head on my bed: I saw, and behold, a tree in the middle of the earth; and its height was great.
DNG 4:11 The tree grew, and was strong, and its height reached to the sky, and its sight to the end of all the earth.
DNG 4:12 Its leaves were beautiful, and it had much fruit, and in it was food for all. The animals of the field had shade under it, and the birds of the sky lived in its branches, and all flesh was fed from it.
DNG 4:13 “I saw in the visions of my head on my bed, and behold, a watcher and a holy one came down from the sky.
DNG 4:14 He cried aloud, and said this, ‘Cut down the tree and cut off its branches! Shake off its leaves and scatter its fruit! Let the animals get away from under it, and the fowls from its branches.
DNG 4:15 Nevertheless leave the stump of its roots in the earth, even with a band of iron and bronze, in the tender grass of the field; and let it be wet with the dew of the sky. Let his portion be with the animals in the grass of the earth.
DNG 4:16 Let his heart be changed from man’s, and let an animal’s heart be given to him. Then let seven times pass over him.
DNG 4:17 “‘The sentence is by the decree of the watchers, and the demand by the word of the holy ones, to the intent that the living may know that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomever he will, and sets up over it the lowest of men.’
DNG 4:18 “This dream I, King Nebuchadnezzar, have seen; and you, Belteshazzar, declare the interpretation, because all the wise men of my kingdom are not able to make known to me the interpretation; but you are able, for the spirit of the holy gods is in you.”
DNG 4:19 Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was stricken mute for a while, and his thoughts troubled him. The king answered, “Belteshazzar, don’t let the dream or the interpretation trouble you.” Belteshazzar answered, “My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you, and its interpretation to your adversaries.
DNG 4:20 The tree that you saw, which grew and was strong, whose height reached to the sky, and its sight to all the earth;
DNG 4:21 whose leaves were beautiful, and its fruit plentiful, and in it was food for all; under which the animals of the field lived, and on whose branches the birds of the sky had their habitation—
DNG 4:22 it is you, O king, who have grown and become strong; for your greatness has grown, and reaches to the sky, and your dominion to the end of the earth.
DNG 4:23 “Whereas the king saw a watcher and a holy one coming down from the sky, and saying, ‘Cut down the tree, and destroy it; nevertheless leave the stump of its roots in the earth, even with a band of iron and bronze, in the tender grass of the field, and let it be wet with the dew of the sky. Let his portion be with the animals of the field, until seven times pass over him.’
DNG 4:24 “This is the interpretation, O king, and it is the decree of the Most High, which has come on my lord the king:
DNG 4:25 that you shall be driven from men, and your dwelling shall be with the animals of the field. You shall be made to eat grass as oxen, and shall be wet with the dew of the sky, and seven times shall pass over you; until you know that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomever he will.
DNG 4:26 Their command to leave the stump of the roots of the tree means your kingdom will be restored to you after you learn that the heavens do rule.
DNG 4:27 Therefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you, and break off your sins by righteousness, and your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor. Perhaps there may be a lengthening of your tranquillity.”

DNG 11:44 But news out of the east and out of the north will trouble him; and he will go out with great fury to destroy and utterly to sweep away many.
DNG 11:45 He will plant the tents of his palace between the sea and the glorious holy mountain; yet he will come to his end, and no one will help him.

DNG 13:1 A man lived in Babylon, and his name was Joakim.
DNG 13:2 He took a wife, whose name was Susanna, the daughter of Helkias, a very fair woman, and one who feared the Lord.
DNG 13:3 Her parents were also righteous, and taught their daughter according to the law of Moses.
DNG 13:4 Now Joakim was a great rich man, and had a beautiful garden next to his house. The Jews used to come to him, because he was more honourable than all others.
DNG 13:5 The same year, two of the elders of the people were appointed to be judges, such as the Lord spoke of, that wickedness came from Babylon from elders who were judges, who were supposed to govern the people.
DNG 13:6 These were often at Joakim’s house. All that had any lawsuits came to them.
DNG 13:7 When the people departed away at noon, Susanna went into her husband’s garden to walk.
DNG 13:8 The two elders saw her going in every day and walking; and they were inflamed with lust for her.
DNG 13:9 They perverted their own mind and turned away their eyes, that they might not look to heaven, nor remember just judgements.
DNG 13:10 And although they both were wounded with lust for her, yet dared not show the other his grief.
DNG 13:11 For they were ashamed to declare their lust, what they desired to do with her.
DNG 13:12 Yet they watched eagerly from day to day to see her.
DNG 13:13 The one said to the other, “Let’s go home, now; for it is dinner time.”
DNG 13:14 So when they had gone out, they parted company, and turning back again, they came to the same place. After they had asked one another the cause, they acknowledged their lust. Then they appointed a time both together, when they might find her alone.
DNG 13:15 It happened, as they watched on an opportune day, she went in as before with only two maids, and she desired to wash herself in the garden; for it was hot.
DNG 13:16 There was nobody there except the two elders who had hid themselves and watched her.
DNG 13:17 Then she said to her maids, “Bring me olive oil and ointment, and shut the garden doors, that I may wash myself.”
DNG 13:18 They did as she asked them and shut the garden doors, and went out themselves at the side doors to fetch the things that she had commanded them. They didn’t see the elders, because they were hidden.
DNG 13:19 Now when the maids had gone out, the two elders rose up and ran to her, saying,
DNG 13:20 “Behold, the garden doors are shut, that no man can see us, and we are in love with you. Therefore consent to us, and lie with us.
DNG 13:21 If you will not, we will testify against you, that a young man was with you; therefore you sent your maids away from you.”
DNG 13:22 Then Susanna sighed, and said, “I am trapped; for if I do this thing, it is death to me. If I don’t do it, I can’t escape your hands.
DNG 13:23 It is better for me to fall into your hands, and not do it, than to sin in the sight of the Lord.”
DNG 13:24 With that Susanna cried with a loud voice; and the two elders cried out against her.
DNG 13:25 Then one of them ran and opened the garden doors.
DNG 13:26 So when the servants of the house heard the cry in the garden, they rushed in at the side door to see what had happened to her.
DNG 13:27 But when the elders had told their tale, the servants were greatly ashamed; for there was never such a report made of Susanna.

DNG 13:29 and said before the people, “Send for Susanna, the daughter of Helkias, Joakim’s wife.” So they sent;
DNG 13:30 and she came with her father and mother, her children, and all her kindred.
DNG 13:31 Now Susanna was a very delicate woman, and beautiful to behold.
DNG 13:32 These wicked men commanded her to be unveiled, for she was veiled, that they might be filled with her beauty.
DNG 13:33 Therefore her friends and all who saw her wept.
DNG 13:34 Then the two elders stood up in the midst of the people and laid their hands upon her head.

DNG 14:5 He said, “Because I may not honour idols made with hands, but only the living God, who has created the sky and the earth, and has sovereignty over all flesh.”
